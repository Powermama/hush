# Verified Messages: one thought, one visual story

## Visual direction

Keep the fragmented robot opening and final download. Between them, use a single composition: “Let your words carry their story.” beside Klee's mechanical birds and a foreground window that follows the same message from local recording to a social post to its signed history. Remove repeated chapter introductions, feature descriptions and the separate privacy chapter.

Palette: midnight #111827, cold paper #EDF3F5, silver #D9E6EC, cyan #8CEDE7 and printed pink #F190C9. System sans carries the headline and social post. Georgia gives the spoken words and their proof a personal scale. The public-domain artwork provides the colour and texture; the foreground UI stays restrained and readable.

Mobile stacks the headline and window with artwork visible around the composition. This is an interactive website illustration, not a video dependency or a live product recording. No new framework, microphone request, external media or generated imagery is introduced.

## Motion and interaction

A finite 15-second sequence shows recording at 0-5s, a social post at 5-10s, and its proof at 10-15s. Start when the window is meaningfully visible, pause offscreen or when the tab is hidden, and stop on the proof. An illustrative cursor approaches the signature before the transition. Manual stage selection and keyboard focus pause playback. Pause/resume/replay controls remain unobtrusive. Reduced motion starts on the final proof and allows all three stages without animation.

The social post uses a compact author row with the shared vector hush mascot as its avatar, display name hushy and handle @heyhush, ordinary social typography, timestamp, views and engagement icons. Its clickable “- verified by hush” signature lives inside the post body, in the same paragraph without a blank line, not in a separate footer or action bar. No edit chip interrupts the message. Clicking or pressing Enter on the signature displays the illustrative proof and moves focus there. The proof shows a concise three-event chronology without an extra disclosure.

## Artwork

The openly licensed Ameca photograph by Willy Jackson remains in six displaced Three.js panels. Preserve source, alteration and CC BY-SA 4.0 credits. Its rendering is on demand with a static fallback. It is not a face scan or authenticity detector.

Paul Klee's public-domain Twittering Machine is locally hosted, recoloured and layered behind the story window. Its source credit stays alongside it. No imagery from the home page is reused.

## Example and truthful limits

Original transcript: “let’s build something that matters”. Manual editing adds capitalisation, punctuation and “together”. Final wording: “Let’s build something that matters, together.” Capture, manual edit and signing have consistent illustrative UTC times. No AI refinement is recorded in this example.

One persistent Illustration label identifies the mockup. The explanatory note, privacy dropdown and recorded-edit disclosure were removed at the user’s request. A short privacy line says audio stays on the Mac; only text and signed history travel encrypted. The proof shows browser decryption with no audio attached. The removed “About this illustration & your privacy” dropdown is not replaced with another explanation panel. Signed history does not establish identity, truth or human presence, and the example's recorded times are not independent trusted timestamps.

Real recipient verification, encrypted storage and immutable download behavior remain unchanged. Detailed privacy terms remain available through the shared footer.
